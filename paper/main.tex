\title[An exact Fourier bound]{An exact Fourier bound with exponent saving $63/50000$}
\date{October 17, 2026}
\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
An explicit finite tensor construction gives a deterministic exact discrete
Fourier transform at every positive integer length $n$, with cost
$O(n(\log n)^{1-63/50000})$. The model permits unrestricted complex
coefficients and one specified root of polynomial order, and charges scalar
preparation, schedule construction, indexing and movement. The construction
uses a 73-axis closure of the public C588AT provider, with arbitrary live
helpers and separately charged initialized copies. Its exact recursive
moment contracts at tensor saving $1261/10^6$; strict exponent slack absorbs
the all-length Fourier transfer loss. This note presents the construction, its exact certificates and its Lean
formalization at the stated exponent saving.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement, model and provenance}
For $n\ge1$, let $\zeta_n=\exp(2\pi i/n)$ and
$F_n=(\zeta_n^{jk})_{0\le j,k<n}$. The model is that of OpenAI's
all-length exact Fourier theorem~\cite{OpenAIExactDFT2026}. Complex field
operations have unit cost. Operations on variable data are linear:
additions, subtractions and multiplication by prepared input-independent
scalars. Scalar preparation may divide only by proved nonzero quantities.
Integer operations and random access on a fixed number of
$O(\log(n+2))$-bit words have unit cost. Preparation, initialization,
table generation, indexing and movement are included. Coefficients and
intermediate magnitudes are unrestricted. One explicitly specified root
of unity is supplied at unit cost; other roots are prepared from it.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Put $\delta=63/50000$. A single deterministic algorithm computes $F_nx$
exactly for every positive integer $n$ and every $x\in\C^n$, with charged
cost
\[
 O\bigl(n(\log n)^{1-\delta}\bigr)
 =O\bigl(n(\log n)^{49937/50000}\bigr)\qquad(n\longrightarrow\infty).
\]
It needs only the specified root $\zeta_{D_*(n)}$, where $D_*(n)$ is the
explicit integer in~\eqref{eq:root} and $D_*(n)<1024n^3$.
Unrolling the dimension-dependent schedule gives exact complex linear
circuits of the same asymptotic size. In particular, $\delta>0.00125$.
\end{theorem}

The proof has four interfaces: a rational provider with verified scalar
and frame identities, a simultaneous tensor program on every live role,
a charged recurrence allowing separate initialized workspace, and a
charged transfer to all Fourier lengths. A finite call histogram by
itself proves none of these interfaces. The complete expanded ordinary
arguments are retained in the accompanying \texttt{proof.md}; the full
finite certificate and three standalone exact checkers are also supplied.
The exposition below follows those arguments without changing their scope.

The all-length reduction and exact-width compiler come from
OpenAI~\cite{OpenAIExactDFT2026,OpenAIFiniteTensor2026}. The second cited
paper's nonuniform subsequence result has different quantifiers and is
not substituted for the all-length theorem. The public complex588
package and its batching framework are attributed to their source
contributors~\cite{SheaPackage2026}. The C588AT data, generalized frames
and compressed closure are taken from the immutable verifier
package~\cite{VerifierPackage2026}; the proof supplies their actual
complex-array semantics and physical accounting.

\section{Explicit subspace frames}
Write $\alpha_0=(1+i)/2$, $\beta_0=(1-i)/2$ and
\[
 C=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_0&\beta_0\\\beta_0&\alpha_0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad C^2=X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad C^{-1}=XC.
\]
For $V=\F^d$, put $D(x)=i^{|x|}$, where $|x|$ is Hamming weight.
Let $P_S$ apply $C$ on the binary coordinates in $S$ and identity
elsewhere. For an invertible binary coordinate map $G$, define
\[
 K_G(x,y)=\mathbf1_{Gx=y}\frac{D(x)}{D(y)},\quad
 U(G,S)=G^{-1}\F^S,\quad T(G,S)=K_GP_SK_G^{-1}.
\]
A frame is a matrix invariant: an array carrying frame $T$ already equals
$Tz$ for its abstract value $z$. Maintaining this invariant does not
instruct the algorithm to evaluate a dense $T$ or its inverse.

\begin{lemma}[Charged frame transitions]\label{lem:frames}
Frames representing the same binary subspace differ by an explicitly
constructible invertible monomial left adapter over $\Q(i)$. Between
nested represented subspaces with dimension difference $0<r<d$, either
direction uses exactly one adapted forward $C^{\otimes r}$ block, besides
monomial work. Under $f$-column replication and $b$ spectator bits, it is
one adapted batch of $C^{\otimes rf}$, and all remaining work, preparation
and storage are $O(2^{df+b})$, with $O(df+b+1)$-bit addresses. Each
complementary fiber of the batch must be charged separately.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The identity $D(x+y)=D(x)D(y)(-1)^{x\cdot y}$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entry}
 T(G,S)(x,y)=\mathbf1_{x+y\in U(G,S)}\alpha_0^{|S|}
 \frac{D(x)}{D(y)}(-1)^{(Gx)\cdot G(x+y)}.
\end{equation}
Suppose $U=U(G,S)=U(H,R)$ and put $v_j=G^{-1}e_j$ for $j\in S$.
Define
\[
 p(x)=\sum_{j\in S}\bigl((Gx)_j+(Hx)\cdot Hv_j\bigr)v_j,
 \qquad Bx=x+p(x).
\]
Then $p(x)\in U$ and $(GBx)\cdot Gu=(Hx)\cdot Hu$ for every $u\in U$.
If $Bx=0$, then $x\in U$ and $Hx$ is orthogonal to all of the coordinate
space $H(U)=\F^R$, so $x=0$. Hence $B$ is invertible. The monomial
\[
 A(x,z)=\mathbf1_{Bx=z}\frac{D(x)}{D(Bx)}
             (-1)^{(GBx)\cdot Gp(x)}
\]
satisfies $T(H,R)=AT(G,S)$ by substitution in~\eqref{eq:entry}.

For nested subspaces choose, by binary elimination, one coordinate map
$B_0$ representing both with nested coordinate sets $I\subset J$.
Change representatives using the adapters just constructed, and use
\[
 T(B_0,J)=K_{B_0}P_{J\setminus I}K_{B_0}^{-1}T(B_0,I).
\]
The reverse move uses $P_{J\setminus I}^{-1}=X_{J\setminus I}P_{J\setminus I}$.
Thus only one forward block is paid in either direction.

All finite binary tables and phases are generated and charged. Tensoring
across $f$ columns collects the selected $rf$ bits by a coordinate
permutation. Enumerate column digits by depth-first traversal of the
complete radix-$2^d$ prefix tree, carrying the image address and phase
product. The tree has $O(2^{df+b})$ nodes including spectators; each edge
costs bounded word work. The same traversal packs selected and unselected
bits. Two array buffers and a prefix stack give the asserted space and
address bounds, without an extra factor $f$ per entry.
\end{proof}

\section{The actual C588AT invocation}
The supplied certificate \texttt{code/c588at-provider.json} has 241357
bytes and SHA-256
\begin{center}\footnotesize\ttfamily
b52b8e31dd731c7bdc1fa3e556224ffa51c76dc66ca36849ecec8c85733f9624.
\end{center}
It is a declarative extraction of the pinned public C588AT data, not an
assumed source theorem. It contains 112 port vectors and orthobases,
588 helper entrance labels, eight retained centers, 1082 RREF frames,
and every rational gate and coefficient~\cite{VerifierPackage2026}.

In an admissible window $V=A\oplus E(\F^9)\oplus D_0$, choose the ordered
basis with $A$ first and the window next. An RREF label $U$ with pivot
rows $b_p$ has involution $G_Ux=x+\sum_p x_p(b_p+e_p)$, taking $U$
to its pivot coordinates. This and the chosen window basis define a
frame $\Phi(U)$ representing $A\oplus E(U)$. The helper's entrance
frame is $E_q=\Phi(U_q)$; its full-window frame $F_q$ uses the same
coordinate map and all window coordinates.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:provider}
The certificate realizes both scalar maps
\[
 (X,Y,S)\mapsto(X,Y+X,S),\qquad
 (X,Y,S)\mapsto(X-Y,Y,S)
\]
on arbitrary live values. Port $t$ starts in frames
$\Phi(\Span u_t),\Phi(0)$ and ends in frames
$\Phi(\F^9),\Phi(u_t^\perp)$. A helper arriving in any frame $Z_q$
leaves in $F_qE_q^{-1}Z_q$, preserving its scalar value. Both maps
use eight separately initialized copies and the same recursive profile
\[
 (h_1,h_2,h_3,h_4,h_5,h_6,h_8)
 =(2568,492,280,232,56,56,92).
\]
The helper residual ranks $9-\dim U_q$ have multiplicities
$\{9:360,8:140,7:32,5:8,4:48\}$. The statement holds under every
column replication, with charged linear remaining work as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:frames}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each listed gate visits a frame before its scalar additions. The checker
verifies strict nested visits, equal labels at every addition, all
orthobases and endpoints, and the entrance-gauged set $G$ of 228 helpers.
The two rational phase matrices are $A_s,B_s$. Let $C_s$ select the
eight retained centers and $J_s$ be the listed scatter matrix. With
$J(X,Y,S)=(X,Y+J_sC_sS,S)$, exact backward covector propagation verifies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar}
 M=B_sJA_s=
 \begin{pmatrix}I&0&0\\I&I&K\\L&0&H\end{pmatrix},\qquad K_{[:,G]}=0.
\end{equation}
The untested initial-$Y$ zero blocks follow from the explicitly checked
triangular gate supports. Every factor of $M$ is invertible; if $Hs=0$,
then $M(0,-Ks,s)=0$, so $s=0$ and $H$ is invertible. Prepare $K,L,H^{-1}$
by finite exact rational operations. The corrections
\[
 g_1(X,Y,S)=(X,Y-KS,S),\quad
 g_8(X,Y,S)=(X,Y,H^{-1}(S-LX))
\]
give $g_8Mg_1(X,Y,S)=(X,Y+X,S)$. The first correction uses only
ungauged helpers at the same frame as $Y$; the last mixes only
full-window $X,S$ arrays, so both are legal pointwise operations.

The scatter cannot directly mix unequal frames. Initialize eight new
arrays to zero, copy the retained helper values at their center frames,
move these copies down to the zero label by eight rank-eight blocks,
and scatter into $Y$. Erase the copies afterward with charged writes.
No live helper is assumed zero. The phase visits plus these eight
blocks give precisely the stated profile: phase $A$ has ranks
$\{1:1028,2:8,4:56\}$, phase $B$ has
$\{1:1540,2:484,3:280,4:176,5:56,6:56,8:84\}$.

Replace each rational shear $I+ce_te_s^T$ by $I-ce_se_t^T$ in the
same chronological order, retaining its frame visits. This computes
the inverse transpose of the scalar product, since
$(DE)^{-T}=D^{-T}E^{-T}$. The corrections remain legal. Realize
$J^{-T}$ by copying $J_s^TY$ at zero frame into the extra arrays,
moving them up to their centers, and adding their negative selected
values to the helpers. The resulting scalar map is
$(g_8Mg_1)^{-T}$, namely $(X-Y,Y,S)$, at the same price.

Finally change the helper's full-window representative to $F_q$ by a
monomial. For arbitrary incoming $Z_qs$, interpret its initial scalar
as $E_q^{-1}Z_qs$; this is semantic interpretation, not a dense inverse
operation. Scalar restoration therefore gives $F_qE_q^{-1}Z_qs$.
The product $F_qE_q^{-1}$ is exactly a monomial-conjugated $P_R$ on
the nonpivot window coordinates, of rank $9-\dim U_q$.

For orthogonal $G$, the ratio $D(Gx)/D(x)$ is a sign character.
Consequently $T(G,S)$ differs from the ordinary product of $C$ along
the orthonormal directions $G^{-1}e_j$ by a monomial: each negative
factor uses $\alpha_0I-\beta_0X_z=iX_z(\alpha_0I+\beta_0X_z)$.
Thus the supplied orthobases give the needed boundary representatives.
All identities tensor across columns. Finite rational corrections,
copy writes and the adapters of Lemma~\ref{lem:frames} are charged
linear overhead. The full gate definitions and exhaustive finite
verification are supplied with the expanded proof.
\end{proof}

\section{The 73-axis closure on every live array}
Let $V=H\oplus E_0\oplus\cdots\oplus E_7$, where $H$ has coordinates
$0,\ldots,8$ and each $E_c$ has eight coordinates. Put $p=0$,
$H'=H\setminus\{p\}$ and $R_{<k}=\bigcup_{c<k}E_c$.
Define the finite nonempty class set
\[
 \Gamma=\{g\in\F^{73\times73}:g^Tg=I\},\qquad Q=|\Gamma|.
\]
Lexicographic enumeration of all binary matrices defines the set and
all its tables constructively. This enormous finite preparation is
charged; $Q$ is fixed independently of the eventual input length.

For port $t$, let $B_t$ be its supplied orthobase, $D_t=\diag(B_t,I_{64})$,
and $\mathcal F_{g,t}(S)=T((gD_t)^{-1},S)$. For each $(g,t)$ use eight
replicas, each containing four pairs $(X_j,Y_j)$, $1\le j\le4$.
Initially $X_j$ has frame $\mathcal F(\{p\})$ and $Y_j$ frame $I$.
These are invariants for arbitrary physical inputs. With 4824 live
helper banks per class, the live count and separate copy count are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stock}
 W=Q(8\cdot4\cdot2\cdot112+4824)=Q\cdot11992,
 \qquad\text{initialized copies}=8Q.
\end{equation}
Serial movement buffers are additional charged storage.

At stage $k=0,\ldots,8$, invocation class $d$ uses background
$d\Span R_{<k}$ and window $dH$. Stage zero serves class $g$ with
$d=g$. For $k\ge1$, let $\Pi_{k-1}$ swap $H'$ with $E_{k-1}$, fixing
$p$ and other coordinates, and set
\[
 R_{t,k}=D_t\Pi_{k-1}D_t^{-1},\qquad d=gR_{t,k}.
\]
Each $R_{t,k}$ is an orthogonal involution, so this is a permutation
of $\Gamma$ for each port. Every data role is used once at its stage.
In the class basis, $(gD_t)^{-1}d=\Pi_{k-1}D_t^{-1}$. Since
$D_t^{-1}u_t=e_p$ and $D_t^{-1}(u_t^\perp)=\Span H'$, the stage-$k$
output frames represent
\[
 X[k]=H\cup R_{<k},\qquad Y[k]=H'\cup R_{<k}.
\]
For $k\ge1$ the inputs are exactly $X[k-1],Y[k-1]$; at stage zero
they are $\{p\},\varnothing$. Same-subspace adapters select the common
$\mathcal F_{g,t}$ representatives. These generator identities hold for
all $g$ by left multiplication, not by an experimental enumeration.

\subsection{A run on four pairs}
Run stage zero forward on pair 1, then stage 1 backward on pair 1.
For $j=2,3,4$ perform the following fixed sequence. Move the idle pair
$j$ to $X[2j-3],Y[2j-3]$, paying rank $16(j-1)$ on each side; do
$X_{j-1}\mathrel{+}=X_j$, $Y_j\mathrel{-}=Y_{j-1}$; run stage $2j-2$
forward on pair $j-1$; move pair $j$ up by rank eight on each side;
do $Y_j\mathrel{+}=Y_{j-1}$, $X_{j-1}\mathrel{-}=X_j$; and run stage
$2j-1$ backward on pair $j$. Run stage 8 forward on pair 4.
Finish pairs 1, 2, 3 at the common endpoints $V,V\setminus\{p\}$ by
rank 48, 32, 16 moves on each side. Finally, for $j=4,3,2$, do
$Y_{j-1}\mathrel{-}=Y_j$, $X_j\mathrel{+}=X_{j-1}$.
Every scalar addition connects equal frames. Before those final gates,
the scalar values are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
j&X_j&Y_j\\\hline
1&-y_1&x_1+x_2\\
2&y_1-y_2&x_2+x_3\\
3&-y_1+y_2-y_3&x_3+x_4\\
4&y_1-y_2+y_3-y_4&x_4
\end{array}
\]
so the final gates give $(x_j,y_j)\mapsto(-y_j,x_j)$.
The extra price per port across the eight replicas is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extra}
 48\phi(8)+32\phi(16)+32\phi(32)+32\phi(48),
\end{equation}
where $\phi(r)$ denotes one rank-$r$ adapted batch.

For the final correction write $\mathcal F(S)=KP_SK^{-1}$ and
$L_0=\{p\}$. The full-subspace adapter gives a monomial $N$ with
$N\mathcal F(V)=P_V$. Also
$\mathcal F(V)\mathcal F(L_0)=J\mathcal F(V\setminus L_0)$,
where $J=KX_pK^{-1}$ is monomial. Apply $N$ to each $X$, $NJ$ to each
$Y$, then replace the physical pair $(X,Y)$ by $(Y,-X)$. The outputs
are $P_V\mathcal F(L_0)x_j,P_Vy_j$, the full tensor applied to the
original physical inputs. This avoids an initial dense frame inverse.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:global}
For every $f\ge1$ and $0\le b<73$, the construction applies
$I_{2^b}\otimes C^{\otimes73f}$ to all $W=Q\cdot11992$ arbitrary
live arrays of length $2^{73f+b}$. Its recursive counts are $Qn_r$,
where $n_r$ is in Table~\ref{tab:rate}. Besides the $8Q$ initialized
copies, it uses fixed serial buffers. All nonrecursive work and
storage are $O((W+S)2^{73f+b})$ for a fixed finite $S$, including table
generation, copy initialization, scalar corrections, adapters, movement
and indexing. Addresses have $O(73f+b+1)$ bits.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
There are 72 invocations per class. Their profiles, the data climbs
in~\eqref{eq:extra}, and the padding give
$n_1=72\cdot2568+504$, $n_8=72\cdot92+112\cdot48$,
$n_{16}=n_{32}=n_{48}=112\cdot32$, $n_{68}=72$, and the remaining
entries $72h_r$. Each rank is below 73; their weighted sum is
$872856=73\cdot11992-2560$. All data and banks have the full tensor
as proved above. Multiplying role and call counts by $Q$ retains the
actual physical construction. Every table has a fixed terminating
generation algorithm. Column-prefix traversal from
Lemma~\ref{lem:frames} implements all replicated monomials in linear
work. Reuse the eight copies per class between substages; initialize
and erase them with charged writes. Fixed correction and movement
buffers are separately counted. No live input is replaced by zero.
\end{proof}

For $1\le t\le2$, $z=(t-1)/(t+1)$ satisfies
\[
 L(t)=2\sum_{j=0}^{39}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}
 \le\log t\le H(t)=L(t)+\frac{2z^{81}}{81(1-z^2)}.
\]
This is the positive integrated geometric series and its tail bound.
Write $73/r=2^st$ with $1\le t<2$; exact rational arithmetic checks
$sH(2)+H(t)\le u_r/10^9$. Since $j!\ge2\cdot3^{j-2}$ for $j\ge2$,
\[
 e^x\le E(x):=1+x+\frac{x^2}{2(1-x/3)}\quad(0\le x<3).
\]
The supplied rate checker verifies the finite rational comparison
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment}
 \Psi=\frac1{11992}\sum_rn_r(r/73)^{1-a}
 \le\sum_r\frac{rn_r}{73\cdot11992}E(au_r/10^9)
 <\frac{999989}{10^6}<1.
\end{equation}
For the actual program, $(Q\cdot11992)^{-1}\sum_rQn_r(r/73)^\alpha$
equals $\Psi$ exactly. This cancels a common multiplicity, not physical
roles or workspace.

For completeness, let $T(k)$ be simultaneous cost on $W$ arbitrary
arrays and $t(k)=T(k)/(W2^k)$. For $k\ge73$, put $f=\lfloor k/73\rfloor$,
$b=k-73f$. Apply the $b$ spectator factors directly, then the concrete
program of Proposition~\ref{prop:global}. A rank-$r$ call has
$2^{k-rf}$ fibers. Recurse on each complete group of $W$ fibers;
use the elementary tensor algorithm on the fewer than $W$ leftovers.
Allocate each child's initialized workspace separately. With
$N_r=Qn_r$, the charged recurrence is
\[
 T(k)\le A_0(W+S)2^k+\sum_rN_r\left[
 \left\lfloor\frac{2^{k-rf}}W\right\rfloor T(rf)
 +B_0(W-1)rf\,2^{rf}\right].
\]
Because $rf\,2^{rf-k}\le rf2^{-f}\le r$, normalization gives
$t(k)\le A_1+W^{-1}\sum_rN_rt(r\lfloor k/73\rfloor)$ with fixed
$A_1$, including scratch and leftovers. For positive $k$, strong
induction on $k^\alpha$, using $rf\le rk/73$, yields $t(k)\le Kk^\alpha$
once $K$ covers $1\le k<73$ and $K73^\alpha(1-\Psi)\ge A_1$.
Dimension zero is identity. This does not use an inequality with
$rf+1$ in place of $rf$.

For a single input initialize $W-1$ additional arrays and keep its
output. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tensor}
 C^{\otimes k}\text{ costs }O(2^k(k+1)^{1-a}),\qquad k\ge0.
\end{equation}
Serial children have strictly smaller integer dimensions, so their
array lengths sum along a stack to less than $2^{k+1}$. Memory is
$O(2^k)$ and addresses have $O(k+1)$ bits. Every child's arbitrary
input may be a parent's nonzero scratch value; none is presumed zero.
The incomplete-batch mechanism is also present in the pinned public
framework~\cite{SheaPackage2026}; the expanded proof states and proves

\section{Charged transfer to every Fourier length}
This section records the complete transfer interfaces of the accepted
proof, following OpenAI's exact-width method~\cite{OpenAIExactDFT2026}.
Part E of \texttt{proof.md} gives the expanded compiler and address

\subsection{A local compiler without extra coordinates}
For primitive $r$th root $u$, let column $k$ of $P$ be the coefficient
vector of $\prod_{j<k}(Z-u^j)$. Then $N=F_r(u)P$ is lower triangular.
With $H_0=1$, $H_j=\prod_{s=1}^j(1-u^s)\ne0$ for $0<j<r$,
\[
 N_{ik}=(-1)^ku^{k(k-1)/2}\frac{H_i}{H_{i-k}}\quad(i\ge k).
\]
Hence $N$ is diagonal--lower-Toeplitz--diagonal. If $D_N=\diag(N_{kk})$,
uniqueness of unit-lower/diagonal/unit-upper factorization and symmetry
of $F_r$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:newton}
 F_r(u)=ND_N^{-1}N^T.
\end{equation}

A fixed-vector convolution has a printed bounded-fan-in/fan-out linear
DAG with $O(v\log(2+v))$ gates and $O(\log(2+v))$ depth, by the binary
FFT. Such a DAG for $M:\C^e\to\C^b$, using $g$ gate coordinates, can
be replayed on arbitrary borrowed values to implement
$(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,y+Mx,z)$. Indeed a forward additive sweep has form
$z\mapsto Uz+Tx$; if the output reads are $Fz+Dx$, then $FT+D=M$.
Sweep, add output, and undo; then sweep with all direct $x$ edges
omitted, subtract its output, and undo. The added $FUz$ terms cancel
and $z$ is restored. This uses at most $8g+2b$ shears. Edge coloring
at each depth level gives $O(H+1)$ disjoint-pair layers for bounded
fan-out. The identity allows borrowed values correlated with all
other coordinates.

To compile a lower triangular Toeplitz matrix $T_v$ with nonzero
diagonal $h_0$, prepare its inverse sequence
$g_0=h_0^{-1}$, $g_k=-h_0^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^kh_jg_{k-j}$.
For $p=\lfloor v/2\rfloor$, factor
\[
 T_v=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\E&I\end{pmatrix}
             \diag(T_p,T_{v-p}),\qquad E=BT_p^{-1}.
\]
Its entries satisfy $E_{ij}-E_{i-1,j-1}=-h_{i-p}g_{p-j}$ away from
chunk boundaries. Therefore each $b\times e$ chunk $M$ has displacement
$M-J_bMJ_e^T$ equal to three explicit outer products, including the
first row/column corrections. The identity
$M=\sum_{\ell\ge0}J_b^\ell(M-J_bMJ_e^T)(J_e^T)^\ell$ evaluates a
chunk with a fixed number of convolutions. Print these graphs and
choose the largest chunk size for which every graph fits with its
source and target in the same $v$ coordinates. A size
$\Theta(v/\log v)$ fits for all sufficiently large $v$; otherwise
use the finite direct fallback. The $O(\log^2v)$ chunk pairs each
take $O(\log v)$ replay layers. Balanced child recursion gives
$O(1+\log^4r)$ layers on exactly $r$ coordinates. All graph printing,
fit tests, coefficient generation and edge coloring are polynomial
in $r$, with an absolute degree. Borrowed coordinates are restored
after each chunk. Equation~\eqref{eq:newton} and transposition preserve
the layer types.

Every scalar shear can use one common pattern of six forward $C$
slots. To see this, put $S_0=\diag(1,i)$ and
$H_2=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Direct multiplication gives $H_2=\alpha_0^{-1}S_0CS_0$ and
\[
 H_2\diag(2,1)H_2\diag(-3,1)H_2
 =K_0=\begin{pmatrix}-8&-10\\0&-6\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For $t\ne0$, conjugating $\diag(-1/8,-1/6)K_0$ by
$D_t=\diag(4t/5,1)$ gives the upper shear of coefficient $t$.
For an arbitrary prepared $\mu$, set $\kappa=1+\mu\overline\mu$.
Both $t_1=\kappa$ and $t_2=\mu-\kappa$ are nonzero, since
$1+|\mu|^2>|\mu|$. Their shears compose to the $\mu$ shear.
Conjugates are prepared by evaluating the coefficient DAG at inverse
roots and $-i$, not by conjugating variable data or testing complex
zeros. This proves the common slot pattern even for zero coefficients.
All needed extra roots have power-of-two order less than $8r$.

\subsection{Synchronization and linear indexing}
For widths $r_1,\ldots,r_\ell\ge2$ and $R=\prod_jr_j$, the mixed-radix
prefix tree has fewer than $2R$ nodes. Tensor monomials are implemented
by carrying original and permuted address prefixes and a running
coefficient. The cost is $O(R)$, rather than $O(R\ell)$.

At a simultaneous $C$ slot each local axis splits into ordered pairs
and singletons. A sector chooses one block on each axis, with sizes
$q_j\in\{1,2\}$; its map is $C^{\otimes k}$ and width $2^k=\prod_jq_j$.
If $p_j$ is the number of local coordinates preceding its block and
$\tau_j=\prod_{i>j}r_i$, its packed start is
\[
 S=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(\prod_{i<j}q_i\right)p_j\tau_j.
\]
This classifies earlier sectors by their first different axis.
During a digit traversal with local coordinate $a_j$ and within-block
digit $t_j$, update
\[
 S\leftarrow S+Q_0p_j\tau_j,\quad Q_0\leftarrow Q_0q_j,\quad
 o\leftarrow oq_j+t_j,\quad I\leftarrow Ir_j+a_j.
\]
The packed address is $S+o$ and the original address $I$. Packing,
unpacking and sector listing therefore cost $O(R)$ in total. The
sector widths sum to $R$ and $k\le\ell$;~\eqref{eq:tensor} bounds
all sector transforms by $O(R(\ell+1)^\alpha)$. Padding local compiler
words to common length gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sync}
 O\bigl(R(\ell+1)^\alpha(1+\log r_{\max})^4\bigr)
       +\operatorname{poly}(\ell,r_{\max},\log(R+2)).
\end{equation}
The polynomial is charged preparation with absolute degree. Local
words are matrix identities on arbitrary states; tensor-product
composition remains valid when other axes alter their borrowed values.

\subsection{Working lengths, CRT and the sole supplied root}
Let $r_j$ be the odd primes in order and choose maximal $\ell\ge0$
with $R=\prod_{j=1}^\ell r_j\le2n$. Choose least $e\ge0$ with
$L=R2^e\ge2n$. Then $2n\le L<4n$ and $2^e<2r_{\ell+1}$.
Elementary prime estimates suffice: the central binomial coefficient
satisfies
\[
 \frac{4^u}{2u+1}\le \binom{2u}{u}\le(2u)^{\pi(2u)}.
\]
Each prime contributes at most $2u$ to the latter coefficient. With
$u=(j+1)^2$ this gives $r_j=O((j+1)^2)$. Since $r_j\ge j+2$,
$\log R=\Theta(\ell\log(\ell+2))$. Maximality implies
\[
 \ell=\Theta(\log n/\log\log n),\qquad
 \log\ell=\Theta(\log\log n),\qquad e=O(\log(\ell+2)).
\]
Trial division finds the needed primes with polynomial logarithmic
work. Initial lengths are handled directly.

For coprime factors $q\mid L$, put $E_q=(L/q)d_q$, where
$(L/q)d_q\equiv1\pmod q$. The CRT idempotents give
\[
 j=\sum_qE_qj_q\pmod L,\qquad
 \zeta_L^{jk}=\prod_q\zeta_q^{d_qj_qk_q}.
\]
Prefix traversal with tabulated residues implements the CRT and local
output permutations in $O(L)$ work. Apply~\eqref{eq:sync} to odd-prime
axes, and the elementary binary FFT to the $2^e$ axis. All tables,
twiddles and compilation are charged, giving
\[
 T(F_L)=O\bigl(L[(\ell+1)^\alpha(1+\log(\ell+2))^4+1+e]\bigr)
                       +\operatorname{poly}(\log(n+2)).
\]
The inverse has the same cost since $F_L^2=LJ$, with $J$ negating
the index. Its only additional division is by the nonzero integer $L$.

For arbitrary $n$, put $\eta=\zeta_{2n}$. The chirp identity
\[
 \zeta_n^{jk}=\eta^{j^2}\eta^{-(k-j)^2}\eta^{k^2}
\]
reduces $F_nx$ to one cyclic convolution of length $L$. Zero-pad
$A_j=\eta^{j^2}x_j$ and take the fixed kernel
$B_u=\eta^{-u^2}$ on residues $-(n-1)\le u\le n-1$, zero elsewhere.
There is no aliasing because $L\ge2n$. Compute two forward transforms
and one inverse, charging the full transform of the fixed kernel as
scalar preparation. Chirps are generated successively in $O(n)$
operations, and padding and pointwise products are charged.

The sole supplied root has explicitly specified order
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root}
 D_*(n)=(2n)L\,2^{\lceil\log_2(16L)\rceil}
            <64nL^2<1024n^3.
\end{equation}
The chirp, CRT, binary-convolution roots and $i$ are powers of this
root; their extraction and local preparation are polylogarithmic.
Every scalar denominator is a root power, a nonzero $H_j$, a nonzero
Toeplitz diagonal, a nonzero integer, $\alpha_0$, or one of the proved
nonzero shear coefficients. All integer quantities and addresses fit
a fixed number of logarithmic words. These checks retain the original
root-supply, coefficient and uniformity conventions.

Substituting the prime estimates gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:before-slack}
 T(F_n)=O\bigl(n(\log n)^{1-a}(\log\log n)^{3+a}\bigr).
\end{equation}
For $\delta=63/50000$, $a-\delta=10^{-6}>0$. With
$t=\log\log n\ge1$, the positive exponential series gives
$e^{(a-\delta)t}\ge(a-\delta)^4t^4/24$, and $3+a<4$.
Thus the extra factor in~\eqref{eq:before-slack} is
$O((\log n)^{a-\delta})$. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
The same program uses a finite direct fallback, with $n=1$ identity;
all choices depend on dimensions and fixed tables, permitting uniform
circuit unrolling. No equality $\delta=a$ is asserted.
